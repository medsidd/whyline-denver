# Data sources and coverage

Sixteen ingestion programs draw from RTD, the City and County of Denver, CDPHE, NOAA, Open-Meteo, the Census Bureau, the FTA's National Transit Database, Veo and OpenStreetMap (street routing for job access). Collection frequency describes our pipeline, not how recently the publisher observed or revised its data. Current schedules/status are dated in [operations](../technical/OPERATIONS.md).

Every source below feeds at least one table in the BigQuery warehouse, defined in the [metrics glossary](METRICS_GLOSSARY.md). The dashboard can query 20 of those tables and two live views; the rest are built on the same schedule but are not yet in the dashboard, and their rows say so.

| Ingested dataset | Configured coverage | Collection and limitations |
|---|---|---|
| RTD GTFS schedules | Stops, routes, trips, stop times, calendars, shapes in the published feed | Nightly extraction. Latest schedule is expanded for analysis; it is not a historical version-matched timetable for every past observation. |
| RTD GTFS realtime | Trip Updates and Vehicle Positions | Since October 29, 2026 a continuous poller reads both feeds every 15 seconds and times each stop visit from vehicle reports (about every 30 seconds); results load every 15 minutes. Before that, 15-minute samples (the snapshot job is now paused). Not every trip reports; on November 7–9, 2026 a poller fault lost November 8 and part of November 9 (see operations). |
| RTD service alerts | Alerts feed: cause, effect, text, and the routes, stops and trips each alert names, with active periods | Snapshotted every 15 minutes by the load job since 2026-11-02. English text only. Alerts describe what RTD announced, not what happened. Used in `mart_alerts_by_route_day`, `mart_stop_closures` and both scorecards, all not yet in the dashboard. |
| Veo scooters and e-bikes | Veo's public Denver GBFS feed: where available vehicles are, by type | Snapshotted every 15 minutes by the load job since 2026-11-02; raw snapshots kept 30 days. Veo has been Denver's only operator since May 2026. The feed is published openly without a licence file; WhyLine publishes only aggregates near stops, with credit to Veo and the City and County of Denver, and never vehicle identifiers. Used in `mart_micromobility_access_by_stop` and the stop scorecard, both not yet in the dashboard. |
| Denver street layers | High Injury Network, transit lanes and signals, curb ramps, traffic signals, bicycle facilities, and the sidewalk asset inventory (each property's frontage: sidewalk or missing, width, surface, condition) | Weekly snapshot from Denver's open-data ArcGIS services. Inventories can lag construction; a missing curb ramp in the data is not proof there is none. Sidewalk condition is from the city's 2022 baseline survey and width is in whole feet. Every layer is used in `mart_stop_walk_safety` (high-injury streets, signals, curb ramps, bikeways, bus lanes and signal priority, and missing, narrow or poor sidewalk near each stop), which also feeds the stop scorecard; transit lanes are also used in `mart_transit_lane_speed_by_week`. All three are not yet in the dashboard. |
| Equity layers | Colorado EnviroScreen 2.0 (CDPHE) for Denver tracts and block groups, with its disproportionately impacted community type; Denver DOTI equity index by block group | Weekly snapshot. EnviroScreen scores are relative percentiles within Colorado. Both are in the dashboard: `mart_vulnerability_by_stop` adds the EnviroScreen percentile, disproportionately impacted status and DOTI equity score of the block group containing each stop inside Denver (about a third of stops; the rest are outside Denver and stay empty). `mart_service_equity_by_group` compares service across these groups and `mart_job_access_by_tract` carries the tract EnviroScreen percentile; both are not yet in the dashboard. CDPHE and Denver are credited on the [attribution page](https://www.whylinedenver.com/attribution). |
| Hourly weather | Open-Meteo hourly precipitation, snowfall, temperature and wind at Denver International Airport | Nightly, the last 10 days; recent hours are forecast-model values until the reanalysis archive catches up (about six days). Modelled, not measured at the station. [CC BY 4.0](https://open-meteo.com/en/license). Used in `mart_weather_impacts_hourly`, not yet in the dashboard; the dashboard's weather table uses the NOAA daily data below. |
| RTD operations reports | RTD's own monthly on-time performance and service availability: all bus, Local, Regional and Regional BRT; light rail, A/B/G and N lines | Weekly read of the PDFs linked from RTD's board briefing documents page, from January 2025. RTD measures at timepoints, using its own definitions; WhyLine measures at every stop, so the two differ. Used in `mart_reported_vs_measured_by_month`, not yet in the dashboard. |
| Jobs by tract | U.S. Census LEHD LODES8 workplace job counts (2023), with OpenStreetMap streets and RTD's schedule for routing | Weekly. Travel times come from the published schedule, not from what ran. LODES adds noise to small counts for privacy. OpenStreetMap is © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL). Used in `mart_job_access_by_tract` and the stop scorecard's jobs within 45 minutes, both not yet in the dashboard. |
| National Transit Database | RTD's monthly boardings, revenue miles and hours, and peak vehicles by mode, from FTA | Weekly snapshot of the full history from 2002. Published about two months behind; FTA revises earlier months. Boardings are unlinked trips: a ride with one transfer counts twice. Used in `mart_ridership_by_mode_month`, not yet in the dashboard. |
| Denver crashes | Denver ArcGIS crash records; ingestor defaults to a five-year source window | Nightly extraction; only the latest extract is used, so crashes older than its five-year window drop out. Location/reporting quality varies. The mart counts a 365-day window ending on the latest crash date in staging, not necessarily today. |
| Denver sidewalks | Inventoried Denver sidewalk segments | Nightly extraction of a slowly changing source. Missing inventory is not proof there is no sidewalk; nearby line length does not verify surface quality, safe crossings, connectivity, or accessibility. This layer's status, surface and year-built fields are empty for every segment (checked 2026-11-07), so WhyLine doesn't carry them; condition, width and missing sidewalk come from the sidewalk asset inventory above. No Denver layer publishes when a sidewalk was built. |
| NOAA GHCND daily weather | Denver International Airport, station `USW00003017`; precipitation, snow, min/max/average temperature | Rolling 30-day collection through yesterday. Publication delays and missing fields vary. Days NOAA cannot supply are filled from Open-Meteo modeled weather for the airport until NOAA publishes them. One airport station cannot describe every neighborhood's weather. |
| Census ACS five-year estimates | Configured 2023 vintage, Denver County tract geography | Re-extracted nightly; annual source releases do not automatically change the configured vintage. Estimates describe a multi-year population, with uncertainty; no rider-level demographics are observed. |
| Census TIGERweb tract boundaries | Configured 2020 boundaries for Denver County | Attempted in nightly static collection as a nonfatal reference-data step. Boundaries are not annually changing observations. |

## Weather missingness

The former Central Park station (`USW00023062`) is no longer the default. The October 2026 repair aligned ingestion and dbt on the airport station and preserved older raw files. Weather units are mm/Celsius. Missing precipitation is null in source data and `unknown` in route-day summaries; unavailable snow can remain null. Comparisons exclude unknown weather, and a missing dry baseline can yield a null delta.

There is no fixed promise that NOAA data becomes complete after a specified number of days. Check the station, metric, and requested dates. See [weather collection](../guides/NOAA_Data_Collection_Strategy.md).

## Geographic and analytical limits

RTD stops/routes can extend beyond Denver County. Census and city infrastructure coverage is narrower; nearby Denver tracts may be associated with a stop within about half a mile, including near county boundaries. Missing values can reflect coverage, unmatched identifiers, incomplete sources, or processing problems and should be investigated rather than assigned one cause automatically.

Crash buffers include all qualifying reported crashes nearby, not only crashes involving transit riders. Sidewalk access and vulnerability scores are relative to the observed dataset. Combining them with reliability does not remove their uncertainty.

The project does not ingest fares or revenue, passenger counts by stop or trip (the NTD's boardings are monthly totals by mode), or verified accessible walking routes. Crowding comes from the occupancy level some vehicles report, not from counts. Bus/rail inclusion depends on published feed coverage; no fixed list of current rail lines is maintained in this document.
